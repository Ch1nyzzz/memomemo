\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \name, a framework for online context curation in automated
harness optimization. Rather than treating a coding proposer's filesystem
access as an all-or-nothing choice, \name makes the proposer's evidence
workspace an explicit control variable. Its iteration-level strategy bounds
and labels historical evidence, while its file-level strategy can be composed
to reallocate read effort within the exposed workspace. Across memory and
software-engineering harnesses, this framing improves held-out performance in
most settings and introduces lower-cost operating points on the cost--quality
frontier. The results do not imply that more curation is always better, or
that the two strategies contribute independently. They show instead that the
proposer's limited inspection budget is itself part of the optimization
problem, and that routing this budget deliberately can make harness search
more effective.
