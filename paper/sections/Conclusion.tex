\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \name, a framework for online context curation in automated
harness optimization. Rather than treating a coding proposer's filesystem
access as an all-or-nothing choice, \name makes the proposer's evidence
workspace an explicit control variable. Its iteration-level strategy bounds
and labels historical evidence, while its file-level strategy can be composed
on top to reallocate read effort within the exposed workspace. Across memory
and software-engineering harnesses, this framing improves held-out
performance in most (benchmark, proposer) settings. On the two memory
benchmarks it opens lower-cost operating points on the cost--quality
frontier; on SWE-bench Verified it instead trades a modest cost increase for
a substantially stronger curated harness. The results do not imply that more
curation is always better, nor that the two strategies contribute
independently. They show instead that the proposer's limited inspection
budget is itself part of the optimization problem, and that routing this
budget deliberately can make harness search more effective.
